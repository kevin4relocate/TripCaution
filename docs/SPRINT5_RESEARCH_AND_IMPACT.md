# TripCaution Sprint 5 — Research Quality and Situation-Specific Impact

**Purpose:** explain how serious a *particular documented travel problem could be if it occurs*, without portraying an entire country or population as dangerous. Severity is potential consequence, **not likelihood**, risk score, live breaking news or an official government advisory. Labels are not generated by Gemini or assigned automatically to past content.

## Four human-assessed levels

| Level | Intended interpretation if the precise scenario occurs | Example research question, not a verified fact |
|---|---|---|
| Not assessed | No explicit human impact conclusion | Legacy live guides and newly imported drafts start here. |
| Low | Limited inconvenience, straightforward workaround | A selected establishment accepts cash only, but another confirmed method is available. |
| Moderate | Meaningful cost or disruption | A particular payment/service restriction causes a missed transfer. |
| High | Potential serious loss, major disruption or personal harm | A specifically documented deceptive service can cause substantial loss. |
| Critical | Potentially life-threatening harm, severe injury or exceptionally serious consequences | A narrow, current and corroborated official warning describes severe consequences for a particular route or activity. |

An everyday payment inconvenience is **not automatically High or Critical**. Likewise, a theft report for one venue does not rate the whole city or country. A high/critical claim must have an evidence-backed impact explanation, a specific applicability scope and genuine HTTPS sources; the owner must explicitly approve it **individually**, including for scheduling. Both the server and the Admin editor prevent high/critical bulk publication. Never equate the number of available source URLs with proof that their claims are true.

## Implemented

1. Per-article `caution_level`, `severity_scope` and `severity_rationale` fields with bounded normalization and an additive D1 migration. All existing records default to `unassessed`.
2. Human editorial controls in Admin: scope + rationale, source checks, explicit one-article confirmation for High/Critical, private preview, audit logging, and fail-closed server-side review gates.
3. Visible, escaped impact panels on assessed published article pages and text-labeled badges on published cards. Topic pages explain the meanings and offer **optional reader impact filters** (filtered pages are noindex). No destination danger leaderboard or unsupported numerical risk scores.
4. New imported drafts and owner-approved replacement imports always start at `unassessed`, **even if an AI JSON payload asks for Critical**. Corrected live articles return to private Review before re-publication.
5. Human editorial recheck reminders default to 30 days; High defaults to seven days and Critical to two days. These are **owner tasks** and not continuous external safety monitoring. Serious new evidence should be checked promptly regardless of the reminder.
6. Research preflight for 1–30 article JSON batches: warns about weak/missing sources, missing claim-to-source records, malformed source mapping and attempted AI severity labels; blocks within-package duplicate titles/slugs. Server import also rejects exact same-country titles with a different slug, but semantic duplicates still need human review. Preflight cannot open links or establish factual truth.

## Editorial procedure for a new article

1. Pick **one traveler problem** and narrow its precise applicability: country, city/venue/route, date, payment product, passport or personal circumstances as necessary.
2. Collect original local operator/authority evidence. If reporting a fraud or personal safety issue, confirm reliable jurisdiction- and date-scoped evidence. Record each material claim against its exact source URL, issuer, and limits.
3. Create a draft JSON with ordinary `research.sources`, `research.uncertainties`, and optionally structured `research.claim_evidence` rows: `{claim,source_url,scope}`. The extra claim evidence is for **preflight checking and the editor's research record**; it does not certify evidence and is not currently stored by the article database. Preserve any full verification notes separately.
4. Run `npm run audit:cautions` on the existing starter package or `node scripts/audit-caution-drafts.mjs path/to/your-package.json`; alternatively use **Import content → Check sources & duplicates** in the private Admin. Correct reported problems and manually investigate warnings.
5. Import to **Review** only. Open the private saved Preview, verify important claims **against original linked pages**, and edit content as needed.
6. If the consequences justify an assessed level, save the *exact scope* and *source-supported reason*. Never infer a level from topic, headline, local stereotypes or the assistant's draft. Confirm high/critical explicitly in the single-article editor; leave uncertain material unassessed or private until resolved.
7. After approval, schedule or publish. Recheck changes through the human editorial queue at the relevant interval. Update or withdraw a materially inaccurate article rather than keeping a dramatic but unsupported warning.

**Research example, not a factual allegation:** compare cash, individual international card products, NETS and QR acceptance at a specifically identified Singapore venue or service. Different merchant and visitor payment instruments are not interchangeable. Unsupported claims about prevalence of pickpocketing or claims that a country is categorically dangerous must never be published.

## Production rollout: mandatory sequence

The code now requires **`migrations/0003_editorial_caution_levels.sql`**. Previous Sprint 4's migration **`0002_global_cautions.sql`** may still be pending in Cloudflare even though its code was merged. Do not assume that GitHub merges have changed your Cloudflare database.

1. Reconcile and confirm the actual Cloudflare deployed Git SHA and migration state. Export remote D1 privately, record its checksum and **test restoring it to a separate staging D1**.
2. On staging, apply any pending migrations 0002 then 0003. Deploy Sprint 5 code to a matching staging Worker and test at least: legacy article without severity, Low publication, High publishing without and with approval, AI import with attempted Critical, revision import to Review, country/topic filters and the review reminder.
3. After staging passes, apply any pending production migrations **before deploying Sprint 5 Worker code**. Recommended owner-run command from a checked-out and authenticated repository: `npx wrangler d1 migrations apply tripcaution-db --remote`; confirm prompts and target. Never store Cloudflare credentials or D1 exports in this public repository.
4. Deploy Worker and re-run private real-session checks, anonymous privacy/SEO checks, and the non-destructive public production smoke. Check that the new published-card badges **only** appear after editor assessment. Validate Cloudflare's deployed SHA, not just the green GitHub run.

**What this phase does not claim:** automatic verification of crime allegations, real-time emergency notifications, a guarantee that high impact means common occurrence, automatic source link monitoring, an email correction inbox already configured, or complete eleven-country researched coverage. A passing static audit only establishes structural hygiene.
